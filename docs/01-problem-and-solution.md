# Problem and solution

## The rural greywater problem

Greywater from kitchens, washing and bathing must be handled close to homes. Land is limited, maintenance is easy
to miss, and a soak pit that works in dry weather can stop absorbing water during floods. Overflow then becomes a
public-health and nuisance issue, while officials have little timely evidence about where help is needed.

## Neerdhara's approach

Neerdhara combines three practical parts:

- A low-cost, gravity-fed treatment unit that does not depend on electronics for water movement.
- A small sensor node that reports level, flood wetness and device health.
- Local workflows for the household, field operator, Gram Panchayat and officials.

The aim is not to automate village infrastructure blindly. It is to make risk visible early, guide the simplest
local response first, and create an evidence trail when trained help is needed.

## Expected value

- Earlier cleaning before overflow.
- Faster assignment and clearer accountability.
- Flood-aware warnings across an affected village.
- Photos and sensor confirmation for completed work.
- Area-scoped information for planning and reports.
